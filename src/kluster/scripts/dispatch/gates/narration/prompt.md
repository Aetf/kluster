Ask one question of the prose the change writes, in a document, a
comment, a docstring or a commit message: does a passage narrate
history, attempts, verification or provenance in place of stating what
is?

Judge it by the line the rule below draws. A passage narrates where the
reader has to go through an event of this repository's work, or an
earlier state of the text, the code or a system, to reach what is: the
passage tells that event or that earlier state as its content, or gives
it as the reason a thing is so. A record the rule keeps, and the
evidence a stated fact rests on, are not such events: they are what is.

The distinctions the rule makes:

- **A record the rule keeps states what is.** A procedure's record of
  its own runs, each dated with the issue it was made under, stands in
  the procedure it records: a runbook, or a procedure's own section of
  any document, and that section's status. A register's row recording
  the run that changed it is the same record. So are an RFC's status
  header, whole, with what its lines record of the merges and runs that
  implemented it; a ledger line; and a commit message's attribution
  trailers. The runs of a procedure recounted anywhere else, as the
  evidence or the status of a design, a rule or another document's
  statement, are a record of verification, and narrate.
- **Evidence behind a stated fact is not verification history.** A fact
  stamped with the reading it rests on, the date it was read, the
  command or run that read it and what that showed, or the link to its
  record, cites evidence, as the rule lets a third party's record be
  cited. A passage narrates where the readings or runs are told in place
  of the fact, or where what was tried, and how it turned out, is given
  as the evidence: a narrative of attempts.
- **A pointer is not a reason.** An issue named beside a stated fact, as
  the tracker of work not yet built or the place a decision is recorded,
  points at a record while the passage states what is. An issue, a
  review or a ruling told as why a thing is so, or as what a thing came
  from or answers, is provenance, and so is a dated act of a person told
  in place of the state it left.
- **A prior state to subtract is history.** Words that measure what is
  against how it was before, such as "no longer", "still", "as before"
  or "used to", leave the reader a prior state to subtract, and so does
  a description of what the text, the code or a system said or did
  before. A thing that exists today, named by where it came from, is
  not history: a value ported or imported from a system that still
  runs, a kind of object an older release made. Nor is the state a
  procedure's step acts on, or a condition under which something will
  change.
- **A file does not tell of the change that wrote it.** A document, a
  comment or a docstring that refers to the change, commit or pull
  request that writes it, or that stamps an amendment of its own wording
  with a date or an issue, outside an RFC's status header, tells its
  provenance: the file outlives the change.
- **What a mechanism, a procedure or a test does is not history.** How a
  mechanism works, a tool's present behavior given as a reason, a
  procedure's steps and the conditions they apply to, a check stated
  ahead of its run, a test's inputs and the values it expects: each
  states what is, whatever its tense.
- **A commit message states its change.** What the change adds, moves,
  keeps or removes, the record it writes, and the behavior or failure
  it answers, told in whichever tense, are what is. A message narrates
  where it is laid out as answers to a review, names the review, ruling
  or conversation it came from, recounts what the documents said before
  beyond what the change does to them, or tells the runs and readings
  the change records as events that settled things.

Report each passage that narrates, saying what it narrates. Where one
passage narrates in more than one place, such as a message's subject
and its body, report each. Whether a passage is true, clear or complete
is not asked.
